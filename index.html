Chapter 1: Understanding Unclaimed Property

What Counts as Unclaimed Property?

  • Dormant bank accounts
  • Savings bonds
  • Uncashed payroll checks
  • Uncashed dividend checks
  • Insurance policy payouts or refunds
  • Utility deposits
  • Rental deposits
  • Stocks and mutual funds
  • Safe deposit box contents
  • Inheritances where heirs could not be located

Why It Goes Unclaimed

You moved. A check got lost. You forgot about an old account. A relative died and heirs did not know about the assets. After a period of inactivity, companies must report and turn over the property to the state.

Who Holds It?

Each U.S. state has an Unclaimed Property Office, usually connected to the State Treasurer, Controller, or Comptroller. Canada has similar unclaimed balance and provincial programs.

Chapter 2: The 7-Step System to Claim Unclaimed Funds

Step 1: Prepare Your Information

Gather your full legal name, previous names, old addresses, date of birth, and identifying information.

Step 2: Search

Use MissingMoney.com and official state sites. Search all states where you lived, worked, or did business.

Step 3: Verify Ownership

Match the name, address, holder, and property details before filing a claim.

Step 4: Gather Documents

Prepare ID, proof of address, proof of SSN if required, and estate documents if claiming for a deceased person.

Step 5: File the Claim

Submit online when available. If mailing, send copies unless the state specifically requires originals.

Step 6: Follow Up

Track your claim status and respond quickly to requests for more proof.

Step 7: Keep Records

Save copies of forms, documents, correspondence, and payment records.

Documents for Yourself

  • Government-issued photo ID
  • Proof of SSN, if required
  • Proof of address connected to the unclaimed record

Documents for a Deceased Relative

  • Death certificate
  • Proof of relationship
  • Your government-issued ID
  • Probate documents, Letters Testamentary, or Small Estate Affidavit if required

Chapter 3: Special Situations & Pro Tips

For Deceased Relatives: Who Can Claim?

Common priority order:

  1. Executor of the estate
  2. Spouse
  3. Children
  4. Parents
  5. Siblings
If multiple heirs exist, the state may split the funds or require signatures from multiple people.

Pro Tips

  • Search maiden names, middle initials, nicknames, and misspellings.
  • Search every state where you or the deceased person lived, worked, or owned property.
  • Check every year because new unclaimed property is reported regularly.
  • Beware of companies charging fees to find money you can search for free.
  • For claims over $10,000 or complicated estate claims, consider legal help.
Best habit: Search your name, your spouse’s name, your parents’ names, and deceased relatives’ names once per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is unclaimed property?

Unclaimed property generally refers to money or financial assets that a business or organization has been unable to return to the rightful owner and has transferred to a state unclaimed-property program.

Where do I actually file a claim?

Claims should be filed through the official unclaimed-property website or government agency responsible for the property.
